# FAQ: Cold Storage and Food Facility Applications

**Audience:** All internal users
**Revision:** 2025-01

## Q: What's your recommended configuration for a -10°F freezer dock?
**A:** The standard recommendation is an **MD-9000** air-powered dock leveler paired with a **ThermaGuard 600** insulated sectional door at the exterior opening. For high-traffic freezer thresholds, add a **RapidRoll 400** interior door for rapid cycle times to minimize cold air loss.

## Q: Why air-powered instead of hydraulic in cold storage?
**A:** Hydraulic fluid viscosity increases significantly at low temperatures, slowing the MD-7000's response and stressing seals. The MD-9000 uses compressed air, which performs consistently down to -40°F, and eliminates the risk of hydraulic fluid contamination in food zones.

## Q: Do we need special finishes for food facilities?
**A:** For USDA-regulated facilities, the standard galvanized finish is acceptable in most dock applications. Stainless steel lip option (+$1,200) is recommended for direct food-contact zones or caustic wash-down environments.

## Q: How do we handle condensation and frost?
**A:** Thermal separation is the primary mitigation. Ensure the insulated door is properly sealed (weather seals, bottom astragal, thermal-break joints verified at PM). A well-configured dock-door combination produces minimal frost; heavy frost on equipment typically indicates a seal failure or an under-specified door.

## Q: What about track heating for freezer doors?
**A:** Heated tracks are available as a factory option on the ThermaGuard 600 for installations with cold-side tracks exposed to freezer air. Specify at order — field retrofit is expensive and often impractical.

## Q: Can I use a RapidRoll 400 as the primary exterior door on a freezer?
**A:** Not recommended. The RapidRoll 400 is not an insulated door — its R-value is effectively zero. It works well as an interior transition door inside a vestibule configuration, or on interior freezer-to-ambient thresholds, but should not be the primary thermal barrier to outside air.

## Q: Does the MD-9000 air bag freeze or stiffen in cold conditions?
**A:** No. The reinforced-rubber bellows is specified to -40°F and has been field-proven in blast-freeze installations. The compressed air supply should be dried (standard FRL practice) to prevent ice formation in the control valving, which is the more common cold-weather failure mode.

## Q: What PM considerations are specific to cold storage?
**A:** Drain the FRL bowl weekly rather than monthly in cold environments (condensation accumulates faster with temperature differentials). Verify door seal compression quarterly. Inspect the dock pit drainage annually — pits in refrigerated zones can accumulate ice if drainage is impaired.
